I

Choice over each page

Full choice. The tool treats the manuscript as the story and your instructions as the direction — you can be as hands-off or as hands-on as you like, page by page.

YOU ASKED

How much choice does the author have over what's on each page? Will the tool just draw based on the manuscript, or will the author be able to provide specific instructions?

1

Manuscript-driven

Write nothing extra and you still get a complete, coherent book. The tool reads each scene and proposes an illustration for every page. The fastest path.

2

Per-page notes

For any page, add a shot list in your own words — “wide shot, warm evening light, cat in the background.” Your note is treated as authoritative.

3

Design locked first

You approve how each character looks before any page is drawn, so your choices carry through the whole book instead of drifting page to page.

The author decides four things on every page

What is on it  ·  who is in it  ·  how it is framed  ·  what mood it carries
— either by letting the story speak for itself, or by adding as much specific instruction as you want.

II

Style, character & font choices

Yes to both. Illustration style, character look, and every text setting are author choices — made from clear samples and previews, not decided for you.

YOU ASKED

Will the author pick the illustration style from a set of samples? And the font, size, and placement of the text?

Illustration style

Chosen from a set of samples — soft watercolour, bold flat colour, painterly storybook, line-and-wash. Once picked, it is applied consistently to the whole book.

Character look

Before the book is built, you approve each character's face, outfit, colours and signature items on a reference sheet. That approved look becomes the single source of truth.

Fonts & text

Font from a curated, print-safe set; a comfortable, adjustable size; and placement into a calm area of the art so the words stay easy to read.

The character is approved before the book is designed

Want the cat orange, the dog in a green cap, or a character with freckles? That is decided and locked at this step — and then honoured on every page. This is what keeps a character looking like the same character throughout.

III

Corrections after a spread exists

A core feature, not an afterthought. Once a page is illustrated you can request a specific change with a simple instruction — and only that change is applied, with everything else kept as it was.

YOU ASKED

How will edits work after spreads are created? Say the author wants a hat on the character, or the cat to be orange, or a jump rope added — can they make those updates and keep everything else as is?

You want…You say…What happens
A hat on the character“Add a red hat to Ella.”The hat is added; her face, pose, outfit and the whole scene stay the same.
The cat to be orange“Make the cat orange.”Only the cat's colour changes; everything else is untouched.
A jump rope in the scene“Put a jump rope in her hands.”The prop is introduced into the existing scene — no redraw from scratch.

Targeted, not a redo

Edits are applied on top of the existing art, so the parts you already approved are preserved. You refine, not restart.

Page by page

A change to one page never affects another. Iterate on a single spread until it is exactly right.

Consistency-aware

If a change should carry through — the character now always wears the hat — it can be pushed into the locked look so every page agrees.

IV

Limits, throughput & upkeep

Straight answers on what the tool is and isn't, so expectations are clear from the start.

YOU ASKED

Any limitations on the final tool? How many books can we create a month, and how much upkeep will be required?

How many books a month?

No fixed cap. A working author can comfortably produce several books a month — more in batches. Throughput is set by your review time and the per-image cost, not by the software.

How much upkeep?

Minimal for you. You supply a manuscript, make choices, review pages. Keeping services current and adding new styles or fonts is handled on the development side. No server to run between books.

Honest limitations

Directed, not perfect first try.

A page occasionally needs one correction pass to land exactly right — which is what the per-page edit loop is for.

Consistency is supervised.

Characters are held identical by design; you approve the look up front so the whole book stays true to it.

Throughput follows budget.

Each illustration is billed per image. Volume scales with review capacity and image spend, not a hidden limit.

Unusual requests take a pass more.

Nothing is off-limits; some highly specific asks simply need one extra round.

V

How a project runs, end to end

The four answers, put together. At every step marked “you,” the decision is yours; the tool's job is to execute it quickly, consistently, and to a print-ready standard.

  1. You provide the manuscript — with any per-page illustration notes you want to add.
  2. You choose the illustration style from a set of samples.
  3. You approve each character's look on a reference sheet — locked before the book is drawn.
  4. The tool illustrates every page, holding characters consistent and placing readable text in your chosen font.
  5. You review and request per-page corrections — a hat here, an orange cat there — until each page is right.
  6. The tool assembles the print-ready PDF at one uniform, professional trim size.

In one line

You stay the author. The tool does the drawing and the production work under your direction — and lets you change your mind, one page at a time, right up to the finished book.

We're glad to walk through any of this live, or to run a short sample — one or two of your pages, in a style of your choosing — so you can see the control and the correction loop first-hand.
