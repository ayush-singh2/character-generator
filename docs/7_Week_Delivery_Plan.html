I

What we are building

A general illustrator engine — a product, not a project. Any manuscript goes in one end and a print-ready picture book comes out the other, with no code changes per book. The consistency is learned into a fine-tuned model, and the whole run is driven from a web app rather than a developer console.

It reads any manuscript

Any story is split into pages, and its illustration notes are treated as binding instructions — nothing about the book is hardcoded.

It learns any cast

Each main character is fine-tuned into the model as its own trained style, so it is painted naturally and identically on every page.

It composes each page

Backgrounds and characters are assembled with a quiet area reserved for the words before anything is drawn.

It sets the words

Captions and bubbles are placed where they can be read, checked for contrast, and kept word for word to the manuscript.

It builds the book

Front matter, story, back matter and cover are assembled at one trim size and exported ready for the printer.

Anyone can run it

A web app shows every page, the PDF and each character's locked look, and lets you upload a new manuscript and generate a book yourself.

The product is the engine — the two books only prove it

General, not two-book-specific.

Nothing is keyed to Ella or Bilbo; every character, scene and layout is derived from the manuscript at runtime.

Proven on the unseen.

The model learns each character, so identity is how it paints — not a correction bolted on afterwards.

The cast is a reusable asset.

Trained character weights and stored rules carry into every future title, in this world or a new one.

Runs as a product.

Upload, generate, review, export — a repeatable service, not a one-off render done by hand.

Days, not months.

The next title starts with the engine built, the rules written, and the cast trainable on demand.

V

Week by week

Each week has one headline goal, one measurable outcome, and two client meetings — a Monday review and a Friday demo — and does not close until the outcome is met or the miss is written down.

WEEK 1 · 25–29 AUG · FINE-TUNEFine-tune the image model, and measure honestly
  • Set up fal.ai access and put the key in place; smoke-test one training call and one generation call before spending on real training.
  • Audit the training set: count images and pose variety per character, and flag anyone too thin to train well.
  • Build the dataset from each locked reference sheet — 15 to 25 varied poses, angles and expressions per character, each with a unique trigger word.
  • Train the first two characters (Ella, Bilbo) on fal.ai, store the trained weights, and wire generation to use them.
  • Build the independent scorecard, freeze today's books as the baseline, and A/B three pages: old method vs compositing vs fine-tuned.
OUTCOME

Fine-tuning works for two characters; on the three test pages identity scores 95 or better and is judged more naturally painted than the compositing engine; the scorecard reproduces the hand review within five points and is saved to file.

MON · REVIEW

Present this plan and the baseline scorecard; agree to fine-tune first, with the web app built in parallel.

FRI · DEMO

The same character in several new poses, old method vs fine-tuned, side by side — identity now locked and painted, not pasted.

WEEK 2 · 1–5 SEP · BUILDINGFine-tuned model becomes the default; web app stands up
  • Train the remaining recurring characters across both books.
  • Make the fine-tuned path the default way pages are generated, injecting each character's trigger word and exact locked spec; keep compositing as a per-page fallback.
  • Now that identity is native, retune the correction step toward composition — scale, grounding, cropping, location and props — instead of chasing identity.
  • Stand up the web app's read-only back end: list books, fetch a book's pages and PDF, and fetch each character's locked references.
  • Scaffold the client's provided web design against one real book, and retire the Streamlit path from the client's view.
OUTCOME

A full page renders end to end on the fine-tuned engine, and the web app loads one real book — pages, PDF and character references — at a shareable link.

Sign off the week-1 A/B results; walk through the client's front-end design exactly as we will build it.

FRI · DEMO

A live web link where the client browses a real book — the first time they see the actual thing in a proper app.

A note on the numbers

The targets are set to be realistic rather than flattering. Week 1's character score is measured on the three A/B test pages, not a whole book, which is why it carries a star. Past the target the remaining calls are made by eye, which is why the final pass before delivery is a person reading the book rather than a score.

VIII

What could slow us down

Each of these is known in advance and has a planned response.

The fine-tune could overfit a thin dataset

Too few or too similar training images and a character only reproduces one pose.

Response. Audit the set in week 1, build 15 to 25 varied images per character, and keep the compositing engine as a per-page fallback.

Training cost or time could run high

Paying to train every character on fal.ai adds up if left unchecked.

Response. Train only recurring characters, batch runs, and track cost per character against the consistency it buys.

Two similar characters could collapse

Look-alikes can blur into each other under one model.

Response. A unique trigger word per character, a paired reference for co-present look-alikes, and a scorecard check that they stay distinct.

The web design could arrive late or change

Building against a design still in flux risks rework.

Response. The back end is design-agnostic and built read-only first against real data, so the design is skinned on top whenever it lands.

A full-book run could surface new faults

A win on three pages may not hold across a whole book.

Response. Always A/B three pages first, and let the scorecard gate every full run before it reaches you.

Late fixes could undo earlier wins

This has happened before, where a fix reopened a problem elsewhere.

Response. The full scorecard is rerun after every batch of fixes, and no measure may slip below its week-5 level.

IX

What you own at the end

The engine and the app come first on purpose, because they are the product — the parts that keep paying on every title after these two. The books sit lower on the list because they are the proof, not the point.

  • The general illustrator engine, able to take any new manuscript and return a finished book with no per-book code changes and only light touch-up by hand.
  • The client web app, deployed, to upload a manuscript, generate a book, browse pages, preview the PDF, inspect each character's look and leave feedback.
  • The fine-tune capability, so any new cast — in these worlds or a brand-new one — can be trained into the model on demand, with the trained weights kept as reusable assets.
  • The independent scorecard, so any future book can be measured rather than argued about, proven already on an unseen manuscript.
  • A short runbook covering how to run the engine on the next title.
  • The two test books at their week-7 targets — no internal notes on any page, correct front and back matter, one consistent trim size — as the acceptance evidence.
  • Every caption readable, contrast-checked, clear of the centre fold and word for word to the manuscript.
  • Print files at print resolution and colour, with correct bleed and trim, signed off by you.
