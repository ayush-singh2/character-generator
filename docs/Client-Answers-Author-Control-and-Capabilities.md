## Introduction

Thank you for the thoughtful questions. They get right to the heart of what makes this tool useful to a real author: *you* stay the author, and the tool does the drawing and the production work under your direction. It is not a black box that swallows a manuscript and hands back a book you have to accept as-is. At every meaningful decision point — the look of each character, the illustration style, the fonts and text placement, and the contents of each page — you make the call, and the tool executes it. After the book is built, you can still go back and change any single page without disturbing the rest.

This document answers each of your four questions directly, in order, and then summarises how a typical project runs from start to finish.

---

## 1. How much choice does the author have over each page?

**Short answer:** Full choice. The tool does not merely "draw whatever the manuscript says" — it treats the manuscript as the *story* and treats your instructions as the *direction*. You can be as hands-off or as hands-on as you like, page by page.

There are three layers of control, and you can use any or all of them:

- **Manuscript-driven (the default).** The tool reads your manuscript, understands who is in each scene and what is happening, and proposes an illustration for every page. If you write nothing extra, you still get a complete, coherent book. This is the fastest path.

- **Per-page illustration notes.** For any page, you can add a specific instruction — a "shot list" in your own words. For example: *"wide shot, Ella kneeling beside the crate, warm evening light, cat visible in the background."* The tool treats these notes as authoritative and builds the page around them. Nothing is left to chance where you've given a direction.

- **Character and world design, locked up front.** Before a single page is drawn, you approve how each main character looks (see Section 2). That approved look is then held identical across every page, so your choices carry through the whole book rather than drifting from page to page.

In practice this means the author decides *what is on each page*, *who is in it*, *how the scene is framed*, and *what mood it carries* — either by letting the story speak for itself, or by adding as much specific instruction as you want.

## 2. Style and font choices

**Short answer:** Yes to both. Illustration style, character look, and all text settings (font, size, placement) are author choices, made from clear options and previews — not decisions the tool makes for you.

### 2a. Illustration style

- You choose the overall **illustration style** from a set of samples — for example, soft watercolour, bold flat colour, painterly storybook, line-and-wash, and so on. You see examples and pick the one that fits your story.
- Once chosen, that style is applied consistently to the entire book, so every spread feels like it belongs to the same title.

### 2b. Character look (approved before the book is built)

This is a deliberate and important step. **Before** the full book is designed, the tool generates a reference sheet for each main character — face, outfit, colours, signature items (a hat, a bandana, a collar). **You approve or adjust that look first.** Only once you're happy does the tool proceed to illustrate the pages, using your approved character as the single source of truth. This is what keeps a character looking like *the same character* on every page.

> If you want the cat to be orange, the dog to wear a green cap, or a character to have freckles, that is decided and locked here — and then honoured throughout the book.

### 2c. Fonts and text

The text on the page is fully under your control:

- **Font:** chosen from a curated set of high-quality, print-safe typefaces.
- **Size:** set to a comfortable, legible default, adjustable to taste.
- **Placement:** text is placed into a calm, uncluttered area of each illustration (open sky, a soft wall, quiet ground) so it stays readable, and the position can be directed per page.

The result is text that sits *with* the art rather than on a plain box stuck over it, while remaining crisp and easy to read.

## 3. Corrections and updates after spreads are created

**Short answer:** Yes — this is a core feature, not an afterthought. After a page has been illustrated, you can request a specific change to that page with a simple instruction, and the tool applies *only* that change while keeping everything else as it was.

Your exact examples are precisely the kinds of edits the tool is built for:

| You want… | You say… | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| A character to wear a hat | *"Add a red hat to Ella."* | The hat is added; her face, pose, outfit, and the whole scene stay the same. |
| The cat to be orange | *"Make the cat orange."* | Only the cat's colour changes; everything else is untouched. |
| A jump rope added to a scene | *"Add a jump rope in her hands."* | The prop is introduced into the existing scene without redrawing the page from scratch. |

A few important points about how this works:

- **Targeted, not a redo.** Corrections are applied *on top of* the existing illustration, so the parts you already approved are preserved. You are refining, not starting over.
- **Page-by-page.** A change to one page does not affect any other page. You can iterate on a single spread until it's right.
- **Consistency-aware.** If you change something that should carry through the book (say, the character now always wears the hat), that decision can be pushed into the character's locked look so every page stays in agreement.

This edit-by-instruction loop is what lets you get each page to *exactly* what you pictured, rather than settling for the first draft.

## 4. Limitations, throughput, and upkeep

We want to be straightforward about what the tool is and isn't, so expectations are clear.

### 4a. How many books per month?

There is **no fixed per-month cap** built into the tool. Throughput is governed by two practical factors:

- **Your review time.** The quality bar is set by *you* approving characters and pages. A book moves as fast as you review it. The generation itself is not the bottleneck; author sign-off is.
- **Image-service usage cost.** Each illustration is produced through an image-generation service billed per image. A typical picture book is a modest, predictable cost, and books can be produced in batches. We can share a per-book cost estimate for your expected page counts.

For planning purposes, a single author working steadily can comfortably produce **several books a month**, and more if pages are approved in batches rather than one at a time. The tool can also work on multiple books without conflict, so volume is a function of budget and review capacity, not a hard software limit.

### 4b. Upkeep

Day-to-day upkeep for you as the author is minimal — you supply a manuscript, make your choices, and review pages. Behind the scenes, ongoing maintenance is modest and handled by the development side:

- Keeping the connection to the image and text services current.
- Adding new illustration-style samples or fonts as the library grows.
- Occasional refinements as the underlying AI models improve.

There is no server for you to run and no software for you to babysit between books.

### 4c. Honest limitations

- **AI is directed, not perfect on the first try.** Occasionally a page needs a correction pass to land exactly right — which is exactly why the per-page edit loop (Section 3) exists.
- **Character consistency is a solved-but-supervised problem.** We hold characters identical across pages by design, and we ask you to approve the character look up front so the whole book stays true to it.
- **Very unusual or highly specific requests** may take an extra correction round. Nothing is off-limits; some things simply take one more pass.

## How a project runs, end to end

To put the four answers together, here is the shape of a typical book:

1. **You provide the manuscript** (and any per-page illustration notes you want).
2. **You choose the illustration style** from samples.
3. **You approve each main character's look** on a reference sheet — this is locked before the book is drawn.
4. **The tool illustrates every page**, holding your characters consistent and placing readable text in your chosen font.
5. **You review and request per-page corrections** — a hat here, an orange cat there — until each page is right.
6. **The tool assembles the print-ready PDF** at a uniform, professional trim size.

At every step marked "you," the decision is yours. The tool's job is to execute your direction quickly, consistently, and to a print-ready standard.
